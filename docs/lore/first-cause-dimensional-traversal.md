## The First Traversal — a campaign-ending hopelessness cliffhanger (user, 2026-09-16)

The moment DT is unlocked is NOT a triumphant upgrade screen. It is a **hopelessness cliffhanger that
ENDS one of the campaigns**, revealed only after everything has already been lost. Beats:

**The setup (hopeless, before DT is even hinted at):**
- The enemy is closing in. The homeworld is surrounded. The weapon that dispensed your doom has
  ALREADY delivered its deadly device, and it is turning the planet into ruins. There is no counter,
  no rescue. The player has run out of options on purpose.

**The cinematic (the campaign's final scene):**
- The Fleet Admiral turns their chair away from the console, the fight already lost.
- A brilliant flash: entire swaths of the homeworld city are turned to ash.
- One brilliant bolt hurtles toward the Admiral, and as it nears it begins to **slow down**.
- The blinding light engulfs the Admiral and their command building (name TBD, the eventual
  command-center location). Everything goes white.
- The Admiral awakens ALONE in a white void, and there discovers the mechanisms for Dimensional
  Traversal. The first traversal unlocks at the exact instant of annihilation.

## The mechanic the beat establishes
- **First DT unlocks at the moment of destruction.** From the white void, you can traverse back to
  just BEFORE that event, and dimensionally traverse FORWARD from that point.
- **Traversal = a knowledge-intact loop (New Game+ shaped).** Each traversal adds additional STORY
  points and you come back with your **knowledge intact**, and potentially with some of what you
  started with carried over, growing with you as your powers grow.
- **You bring more each time.** The first traversal lets you bring CAPTAINS with you; each subsequent
  traversal lets you bring more / accomplish more, because you are steadily growing more powerful and
  can carry more forward. This escalation IS part of the storyline and how it unfolds.
- Ties into the prestige/scaling architecture (multiplicative-at-prestige) and the meta-achievement
  tier (DT-spanning milestones) already logged in SUGGESTIONS.

## Dimensional Traversal: the scaling + per-dimension system (from the 2026-08-21 design pass)

Ported here from memory [[project_fleet_admiral_first_cause_dt]] into the git-durable doc (the
original full prose lived in an ephemeral scratchpad and is gone; this is the recorded substance).
⚠️ If detail is missing versus what was originally typed, the user should flag it and we re-add it.

- **DT is the prestige BACKBONE and the STORY spine, not a number faucet.** A Torment-tier ladder,
  DT I..X.
- **Scaling difficulty:** number inflation is COSMETIC (both sides balloon, enemies ~x2 / player
  ~x1.75 per tier) with a small controlled NET delta (~10-25% harder per tier) that is the REAL
  difficulty. The design UNIT is WIN RATE (a fresh destroyer ~100% at DT0 -> ~85% at DT1), measurable
  with the existing combat-1.0 harness (resolvePatrolWaves / forecast / patrol-balance).
- **Each dimension is UNIQUE (the "new hazards / changes as you go from dimension to dimension"):**
  every DT is its own dimension with SYMMETRIC world-modifiers grounded in real physics (fine-tuned
  constants + matter/antimatter Big-Bang seeding). Each grants a prestige-tree point plus a powerful
  buff (buffs-as-VERBS, e.g. "5% duplicate-on-craft"), and GATES future systems (the 2.0 dreadnought /
  "FA operations" unlock around DT V).
- **Dimension-Shifted gear = the endgame loot chase** (a flat base +% is just seasoning).
- **Architecture:** ONE type-aware CENTRALIZED scaling seam (magnitudes vs probabilities scale
  differently, so +5% crit does not warp the crit meta); ADDITIVE so DT0 is today's game
  byte-identical (existing saves + the combat debut untouched until the first transit). Derived
  readouts (Battle Rating, Threat Assessment) are the HONESTY layer so cosmetic big numbers never mask
  the true net delta.
- Ties to the prestige/scaling architecture in SUGGESTIONS (additive-within-prestige,
  multiplicative-at-prestige) and the Safety-Interlocks endgame risk axis.

## Threat escalation: LEVIATHANS (user, 2026-09-16, a colleague's idea)
The First Cause / DBDs (name TBD) do not only field normal-sized troops. As the situation grows
progressively worse, they deploy **Kaiju / Leviathan-scale entities** as part of what they throw at
you: a visceral signal that the threat is massive and escalating. In gameplay these appear as random
boss encounters during exploration surveying (a cascading, prepared-only, escape-if-you-must away-team
fight); the full mechanic lives in the Exploration saga doc, Part D.1 (Away-Team combat). Lore-wise,
the Leviathans are one of the antagonist's escalation FORMS, sitting alongside the 5 escalating Modes
already recorded in memory [[project_fleet_admiral_first_cause_dt]].

## Story hook: the Archive AI and the dead ship (user, 2026-10-01)
The **Intact Data Core** (a salvage-only exotic) is "a sealed memory core pulled whole from a dead ship,
its archives locked behind a cipher no one alive can key." The user's hook: the AI in those archives can
be brought back, **coalescing into the tangible world** inside an environment built from Data Cores, and
taken on as a **unique Senior Staff officer**. It may **know something about the DBDs**: the ship it served
on is dead, and it may have witnessed what killed it. That makes the Data Core a quiet thread toward the
First Cause long before the player knows the enemy exists (its in-game flavor already ends with a crew
member asking "What is this thing even for?", the game's first meme). Mechanics are SAVED FOR LATER (see
SUGGESTIONS "THE ARCHIVE AI"). ⚠️ The namelessness rule applies: the AI must never NAME the DBDs before
the story does; it can know, hint, fear, or refuse to say.

## Dimension idea: the Sun pushed into a red giant (user, 2026-11-18)
User, verbatim: "We should have something happen to Earth. A device launched into our sun that pushes the sun's
evolution into the next phase, swelling it into a red giant and causing a mass extinction event. Easy to play into the
DR lore and so forth, but I don't know how it might impact the actual game itself unless there's some tie in with
Earth that we don't have in that DR that makes the game harder."
("DR" = one of the Dimensional Traversal dimensions, DT I..X.)

Existing game ties to Earth and Sol that this dimension could act on (found 2026-11-18, all PROPOSALS, nothing
decided):
- **Crew supply.** The saga design says passive crew arrive "on TRANSPORTS from Earth and nearby colonies"
  (exploration-crew saga doc, section 3). ~~With Earth gone, only the colonies send people: fewer recruits, harder to
  staff ships.~~ SUPERSEDED by the user (2026-11-18, below): crew arrivals SPEED UP (a refugee crisis), not dwindle.
- **The Earth-born race** (crewRaces.ts: the balanced default origin, racial = Crew Effectiveness). No new Earth-born
  recruits in this dimension; only those already serving remain.
- **The rest of Sol's human origins.** Real physics: a red giant scorches Mars and boils off the Belt's ices, while
  the habitable zone moves outward toward the outer planets. Martian and Belter recruits could dwindle while
  Outer-Colony recruits rise. That makes the modifier SYMMETRIC (some origins lose, one gains), matching the
  per-dimension rule above.
- **The homeworld sits near Sol** (galaxy.ts `PROVISIONAL_HOMEWORLD`, "provisionalNearSol"; Tier I = the Local
  Bubble). A swollen Sun next door could be a near-space hazard or a new survey site.
- **Story rhymes:** the master design's candidate driving force "The long search: Earth is lost" (master design
  5.5), and the First Traversal's "deadly device" that ruins the homeworld. Whether the Sun device is the same weapon
  is the user's call; do not connect them until they say so.

User follow-up (2026-11-18, verbatim): "Ooooh. Yes. I like that. In that DR, we have to develop a shielding that can
help protect us from the sun, and try and solve the mystery of what happened to the sun, and to explore the Sol System
after the issue transpires. The sun is not large enough to supernova, so it will only turn the entire system into a
barbecue."

The dimension's three player goals (user's words, DECIDED as the shape):
1. **Develop shielding** that protects us from the Sun.
2. **Solve the mystery** of what happened to the Sun.
3. **Explore the Sol System** after the event.

Physics rule (user): the Sun is too small to go supernova (that takes roughly 8 or more solar masses). It swells into
a red giant and bakes the system; no explosion.

Notes for the design pass (PROPOSALS, not decided):
- "Shielding" here is protection from the Sun, separate from ship combat shields (which already exist). Keep the two
  clearly apart in names and stats.
- The three goals line up with systems that exist or are planned: research (shielding), story and quests, planned for
  0.15.0 (the mystery), and expeditions (exploring Sol).
- Real stellar aftermath, if later beats want it: red giant, then a helium flash, later a shed shell of gas (a
  planetary nebula), ending as a white dwarf.
- ~~Still open: slow squeeze or sudden loss of crew?~~ ANSWERED by the user (below): neither; crew speeds up.

User follow-up 2 (2026-11-18, verbatim): "The crew actually speeds up, and colony missions increase. It'd become a
refugee crisis."

DECIDED (user):
- **Crew arrivals speed up** in this dimension: people flee Sol, so the homeworld takes in MORE crew, not fewer.
- **Colony missions increase.**
- The framing is a **refugee crisis**.

Ties found 2026-11-18 (facts):
- Evacuation / transport / colony missions are PLANNED, not built: the saga design lists `crewCapacity` /
  `maxCapacity` as ship stats that "FUTURE evacuation/transport/colony missions gate on" (saga doc ~line 311;

  explorer was designed with "enormous evacuation space" for exactly this.
- So this dimension needs those missions to exist first; it is their natural showcase.

Open for the design pass (PROPOSALS / QUESTIONS, not decided):

- "Crisis" vs the game's calm promise ("build ease-of-use, sell peace"): should the refugee load be a pressure that
