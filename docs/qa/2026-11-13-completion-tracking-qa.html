What it does. Every system an expedition visits gets a completion percentage. The Expedition Console shows it under the system's name ("Bodies 2/3 · Objectives 3/5 · 50%" with a small ring) and on each body card; the Resume card shows the same number; a candidate visited before shows "Visited: N%" or the badge; at 100% the claim card says so. The Archive's Survey Log tab opens with a Systems section listing every system visited since the update; the Subspace Telescope's galaxy map puts a green arc around each region badge for the share of its systems completed. Nothing rewards it.

Where to test. Staging, devpreview.crystalisoft.com, on the 0.14.0 build, with your own migrated save. Launch an expedition and watch it through; recall one after a body is charted for the Resume rows; abandon one for the Abandoned mark.

Builder notes (completion tracking, 2026-11-13)

  1. One save change, optional. systemRecords (one record per system: its id and name, tier, region stamp with bearing and distance, each body's scan and objective counts, the nebula sweep, when, and "abandoned") is written at a claim and at an abandon. Because your rule weighs each body equally, the record keeps the counts per body (the mockup's notes listed totals, written before that answer). The % is always derived, never stored. No save version change.
  2. The nebula sweep belongs to no body, so it is weighed as one more equal share of the system (whole once swept). Your answer counted it as "one objective of the system" without saying its weight: flagged as an open question.
  3. A recalled run whose system goes to Resume writes no record at its claim: the system is listed live (In Resume) until it is finished or abandoned. A run recalled before anything was charted is a candidate again, not listed.
  4. The hidden ledger (notableEvents) records "system completed" at the claim of a 100% run and "relic set completed" when a study completes an exceptional set. No screen.
  5. One new glyph, systemComplete (an orbit with a planet riding it and a check inside), always beside the word Completed except on the map, where the legend names the arc.
