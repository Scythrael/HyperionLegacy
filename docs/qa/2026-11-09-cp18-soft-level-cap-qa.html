What it does. At or below level 30, a level costs what it always did: 300 x level. Past level 30 the cost is 300 x level x 1.08(level - 30), rounded to a whole number. So level 30 still costs 9,000; level 31 costs 10,044 (it was 9,300).

Builder notes (CP-18, 2026-11-09)

  1. The threshold. The cost to go from level 30 to 31 is the threshold level's own cost, so it is unchanged (9,000). The first level that costs more is 31 to 32. Levels 1 through 30 are byte-identical to before (the golden rows prove it).
  2. Stored experience on an old save. Kept as it is, with no migration and no SAVE_VERSION bump (still 71). The new price is never below the old one, so a save cannot gain a level from this change. The stored number stays progress toward the next level, so nothing is lost; the bar reads lower.
  3. Experience above the old price. Only the fold's own carry (10,000 level-ups in one call) can leave that. It spends at the new price on the captain's next award, one level per price paid, with no special case.
  4. Rounding. The factor is multiplied up one level at a time and the cost rounded once, not computed with a power function, whose last digit the language leaves to each browser. Every cost is a whole number and is checked against exact arithmetic through level 266.
  5. Very high levels. Costs are safe whole numbers through level 356. Decimal experience keeps whole-XP steps honest to about level 330 (XP around 1e15), which is millions of years per level at 1 XP a second, so no captain gets there. Past what a number can hold (about level 9,060) the price stays at the largest number, so the level still comes.
  6. Unaffected. The level 25 retirement gate, one talent point per level, the fold, and the Fleet Admiral and Crafting curves.
  7. Wiring. A new live row, captain:progression:softLevelCap, measures what a level-up really drains on a flown second at level 30 and level 40.
